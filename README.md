## Literature Review

The growth of advanced analytics in Major League Baseball has transformed how organizations evaluate potential players. Statistics influence long-term contract decisions made by organizations that pose financial risks. One variable that needs to be more considered in these organizational decisions is the relationship between aging and player performance. If organizations can understand the relationship between these two variables, it will lead to rational decisions that stabilize team success as well as financial stability. As contracts continue to grow larger every year, aging and player performance is becoming the hottest importance for ownership groups of organizations to consider.

Peak player performance is an important variable for ownership groups to consider when signing an individual player to a lengthy contract. Research has demonstrated that peak athletic performance for Major League Baseball batters was at the age of 26.6 years (Ng, 2017). This suggests that when players enter their age-27 season they are likely approaching a decline in athleticism. When players gain more experience from playing in Major League Baseball at a younger age, their peak overall performance is around the age of 29 years old (Ng, 2017). Peak athletic performance as well as gained experience from playing in the Major League Baseball extend a batters' prime years. Peak athletic performance and experience is important to consider when managers and owners are offering lengthy luxurious contracts to batter's in Major League Baseball. 

Lengthy luxurious contracts in Major League Baseball are offered to players who have great statistical accomplishments. Statistical performance has been the main factor in signing players long-term contracts in Major League Baseball (Cahill, 2014). Ownership identifies statistical performance as the most important factor while expereince and age are also important. Research demonstrates that an equal amount of players improve and decline statistical performance after signing a long-term contract but less-experienced players are more rewarding to sign as they have a higher rate of positive change compared to older experienced players (Cahill, 2014). Younger, less experienced players have the opportunity to grow their statistics more than older experienced players. Understanding the relationship between age, experience, and statistical performance are important for ownership groups when they consider signing a player to a long-term contract. 

Guaranteeing a Major League Baseball player millions of dollars over many years is a risk ownership groups take. Long-term contracts in Major League Baseball are an investment made that costs well over 100 million dollars. This amount of money offered to a single player for a team poses tremendous risk as contracts in Major league baseball are fully guaranteed (Hagan, 2017). Many organizations in Major league Baseball offer plenty of large contracts to players even though they do not work out in succesful outcomes. Research demonstrates that only 29.7% of long-term contracts in Major League Baseball were deemed successful (Hagan, 2017). Organizations should consider this statistic when offering large long-term contracts as they could be wasting millions of dollars on a single investment. If an organization is going to offer a long-term contract for a player then understanding the relationship between age and performance could help make better decisions. 

Based on academic research, this project seeks to reveal the relationship between age and batting statistics for player in Major League Baseball. The research will demonstrate the patterns and trends in the relationship that may help ownership groups make decisions that enhance both team performance and organizational value.
